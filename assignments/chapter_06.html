Why this exercise fits: Chapter 6 specifically develops Euclid, Extended Euclid, Euler's phi function, and Euler/Fermat relationships.

Learning Objectives

  • Compute gcd values.
  • Find modular inverses.
  • Compute phi(n) for small n.
  • Verify Euler's theorem experimentally.

Programming Assignment

Build a small number-theory toolkit with functions for gcd, extended gcd, modular inverse, phi(n), and modular exponentiation. Use it to test whether a^phi(n) mod n equals 1 when gcd(a,n)=1.

Requirements

  • Implement all five functions yourself except basic I/O.
  • Read a and n.
  • Report gcd(a,n), phi(n), and the modular inverse when it exists.
  • Verify Euler's theorem when applicable.

Example / Guidance

a=7, n=26\ngcd=1\nphi=12\ninverse=15\n7^12 mod 26 = 1
